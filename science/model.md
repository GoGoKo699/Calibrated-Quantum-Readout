# The calibrated readout problem

A source prepares copies of a quantum state near a known operating point. A readout must be chosen before deciding which small change of the state will matter. The objective is to retain as much local sensitivity as possible in the least favorable direction, using one copy at a time.

The quantum Fisher metric provides a natural scale for comparing directions. It measures the information available when a measurement can be optimized separately for one specified direction. A common measurement generally retains only a fraction of that information when it must serve all directions simultaneously. The problem here is to maximize this worst-direction fraction at a calibrated state.

## 1. States and local perturbations

Let $`\mathcal H=\mathbb C^d`$ and let $`\rho`$ have exact rank $`r`$, with $`1\le r\lt d`$. Choose an occupied eigenbasis and write

```math
\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_j|e_j\rangle\langle e_j|,
\qquad \lambda_j\gt0,\qquad \sum_j\lambda_j=1.
```

Let $`P`$ project onto its support and $`Q=\mathbb 1-P`$ onto its kernel. The eigenvalues may be arbitrarily unequal. Degeneracies among the positive eigenvalues are allowed; any orthonormal eigenbasis inside a degenerate eigenspace is suitable.

The model is the smooth manifold $`\mathcal M_r`$ of trace-one states of exactly rank $`r`$. Its real tangent space is

```math
T_\rho\mathcal M_r
=\{X=X^\dagger:\mathrm{Tr}\,X=0,\ QXQ=0\},
\qquad \dim T_\rho\mathcal M_r=r(2d-r)-1.
```

The occupied block can change arbitrarily subject to trace zero, and the support can rotate into the kernel. Every displayed tangent is physically realizable by a smooth constant-rank curve. In block form, write $`X=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\B^\dagger&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$ and $`D=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)`$. A trace-one positive occupied block $`D+tA`$ and a unitary rotation generated by

```math
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&-D^{-1}B\\B^\dagger D^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}
```

give $`\rho(t)=e^{tK}[(D+tA)\oplus0]e^{-tK}`$ with derivative $`X`$ at zero. Positivity holds for sufficiently small positive and negative $`t`$ because $`D`$ is positive definite.

Exact rank is a local promise. A state of rank $`r`$ at an isolated point of a rank-changing family can have different second-order information behavior and is outside this smooth-manifold statement.

## 2. Information conventions

The symmetric logarithmic derivative associated with $`X`$ solves $`2X=\rho L_X+L_X\rho`$. Its kernel-to-kernel block does not affect the metric. Define

```math
J_\rho(X,Y)=\mathrm{Re}\mathrm{Tr}(\rho L_XL_Y).
```

In the chosen eigenbasis,

```math
J_\rho(X,X)
=\sum_{j\le r}\frac{X_{jj}^2}{\lambda_j}
+4\sum_{i\lt j\le r}\frac{|X_{ij}|^2}{\lambda_i+\lambda_j}
+4\sum_{j\le r,\,a\gt r}\frac{|X_{ja}|^2}{\lambda_j}.
```

This form is positive definite on the full real tangent. The term *quantum Fisher information* below always means this ordinary pointwise SLD metric.

For a discrete POVM $`M=\{E_x\}`$, its classical score Fisher information is

```math
p_x=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho E_x),\qquad
I_M(\rho;X,Y)=\sum_{x:p_x\gt 0}
\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(E_xX)\mathrm{Tr}(E_xY)}{p_x}.
```

A zero-probability effect has zero first derivative on this two-sided tangent and contributes zero to ordinary score information. The attaining POVM has strictly positive nominal probabilities, so its own experiment is locally regular. The [converse](optimality.md) treats arbitrary outcome spaces using the finite trace measure of a POVM. Direction-dependent nonregular limits at zero probabilities are not substituted for a common Fisher matrix.

## 3. Measurement resources and quantifiers

One readout acts on one input copy. Ancillas prepared independently of the unknown local perturbation, general known quantum controls, recorded randomness and within-copy adaptation are allowed: together they induce a single-copy POVM. No coherent operation joins different input copies in the construction.

The apparatus is calibrated at the nominal state $`\rho`$. It may depend on its eigenbasis and rank. Once selected, its effects are held fixed when the unknown state is varied and probabilities are differentiated. The construction does not require the positive eigenvalue values, the coordinates of a subsequent submodel, or its chosen loss function.

The exact optimization is

```math
c_*(\rho)=\sup_M\ \inf_{0\ne X\in T_\rho\mathcal M_r}
\frac{I_M(\rho;X,X)}{J_\rho(X,X)}.
```

The same measurement must serve every direction in the inner infimum. Choosing a different optimal measurement after learning $`X`$ would be a different optimization. In local coordinates with nonsingular $`J`$, this is equivalently

```math
c_*(\rho)=\sup_M\lambda_{\min}
\left(J^{-1/2}I_MJ^{-1/2}\right).
```

The expression is coordinate invariant. It is the E-optimal design criterion applied after normalization by the quantum Fisher metric; general quantum optimal-design terminology is reviewed in [Suzuki20](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#suzuki20).

## 4. Exact rank law

**Theorem.** For every state of rank $`r`$ on $`\mathbb C^d`$, with $`1\le r\lt d`$,

```math
c_*(\rho)=\frac{1}{2r}.
```

A finite rank-one POVM with $`r(2d-1)`$ outcomes attains the supremum. All of its outcomes have positive probability at the nominal state. Its Fisher matrix obeys $`I_M\succeq J/(2r)`$ on the entire rank-$`r`$ tangent, including occupied eigenvalue changes, occupied coherences and support motion.

The [construction](construction.md) proves a stronger exact quadratic identity. The [optimality proof](optimality.md) uses the inherited refined Gill–Massar bound from [ZC26](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#zc26). The bound on the $`2r(d-r)`$ support-rotation directions already prevents any common measurement from exceeding $`1/(2r)`$ in its worst direction.

The pure-state case $`r=1`$ reproduces the established Fisher-symmetric result [Li16](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#li16). The intermediate-rank conclusion concerns arbitrary positive occupied spectra. An all-direction Fisher guarantee pulls back to every smooth fixed-rank submodel. Exact equality for $`c_*`$ is asserted for the full tangent model; a restricted submodel can admit a larger fraction.

## 5. Consequences and boundaries

For an identifiable parameter model, matrix inversion gives

```math
I_M^{-1}\preceq2rJ^{-1}.
```

Thus one calibrated measurement supplies a common local covariance guarantee for every positive semidefinite quadratic cost. The [estimation account](estimation.md) gives an actual locally unbiased score estimator and the pointwise adaptive interpretation, including nuisance parameters. It also distinguishes this sharp Fisher-retention factor from the generally different ratio between optimal collective and individual estimation costs.

The factor contains no ambient-dimension or spectral-condition-number penalty. Physical preparation of the apparatus, however, requires a calibrated eigenbasis. The [resource account](resources.md) charges this input and explains why rare-eigenvector calibration can remain difficult. No efficient many-qubit gate synthesis or spectrum-uniform finite-sample calibration follows from the outcome count.

The exact law requires $`r\lt d`$. When $`r=d`$ there is no support-to-kernel tangent, so the matching converse disappears. At a full-rank maximally mixed state, the established optimum is $`1/(d+1)`$, which exceeds $`1/(2d)`$ [ZH18](../references/RELATED_WORK.md#zh18). Adding a previously unoccupied physical level while allowing unknown motion into it changes the tangent model and therefore can change the optimization.

The [rank-two qutrit](examples.md) makes these distinctions explicit. The [related-work comparison](../references/RELATED_WORK.md) separates the inherited measurement ingredients and converse from their finite all-spectrum attainment.
